ChordLens

Reading guitar chords from the shape of your hand

standby

The classifier never sees your camera. Every frame is reduced to 21 hand landmarks, those landmarks become 85 geometric measurements, and a logistic regression model trained on 12,823 frames decides which chord you are holding. All of it happens inside this browser, and nothing is ever uploaded.

Camera idle

Position your fretting hand so the camera can see it clearly, then hold a chord. Everything is processed on this device, and the video is never sent anywhere.

Measured accuracy

79.4% on recording sessions the model had never seen, and 68.9% on a player whose data was withheld from training entirely. That second figure is 6.2× the 11.1% chance level.

Nine chords

A, B, C, D, Dm, E, Em, F and G. The five remaining minor chords are set aside because each one repeats the shape of another chord further along the neck, and these measurements are designed to ignore where the hand sits.

A caveat

The figures above were measured using the Python version of MediaPipe. This page runs the JavaScript build instead. The classifier is identical in both, but the landmark detector is a different release, so what you see here may not match the published numbers exactly.
